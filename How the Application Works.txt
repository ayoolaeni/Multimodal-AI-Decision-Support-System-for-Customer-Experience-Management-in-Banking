HOW THE APPLICATION WORKS
(Simple Q&A notes, saved from a conversation with Claude)

============================================================
Q1: It's working, how can I operate the app to see the function?
    (Explain to me like a baby)
============================================================

1. OPEN IT
The app runs in a web browser. Open Chrome/Edge and go to:
    http://localhost:8502   (started with docker compose)
    (or http://localhost:8501 if started without Docker)

2. WHAT YOU WILL SEE
A page titled "Multimodal Customer Experience Decision Support" with:
- A yellow warning banner at the top: "Recommendations only" — reminding
  everyone the app never acts on its own, a human officer decides.
- A SIDEBAR on the left with filter controls.
- A big list in the middle: "Ranked worklist" — a list of (fake/synthetic)
  bank customers, sorted from most urgent to least urgent.

3. HOW TO EXPLORE A CUSTOMER
Each customer shows as a collapsed bar, like:
    #1 · CUST00042 · risk 0.87 · dominant: Written complaint/chat

- #1        = their rank (1 = most urgent)
- risk 0.87 = risk score from 0 to 1 (higher = more concerning)
- dominant  = which evidence type (text, voice, or transaction) drove the
              score the most

Click on the bar to expand it. Inside you'll see:
- The recommended next action (e.g. "Escalate to fraud team")
- A breakdown of WHY — a score for each of the 3 evidence types
  (Text / Voice / Transaction)
- Snippets of the actual complaint text or call transcript, if available
- On the right: a radio button where the "officer" can mark the customer
  as "Act on recommendation" or "Dismiss" — for the demo only, does
  nothing real.

4. SIDEBAR FILTERS (left side)
- Minimum risk score slider — drag up to only show riskier customers
- Segment — filter by customer type (mobile app user vs USSD user)
- Dominant signal — filter by which evidence type is driving the flag
- Hide dismissed — hides ones already marked "Dismiss"

5. THAT'S IT
It's a read-only dashboard — nothing else to "run." Scroll through the
list, click a few customers open, try the filters. That shows the whole
feature: combining text + voice + transaction data into one ranked,
explainable list for a human to review.

Q2: The AI is the one giving the recommendation, e.g. "Recommended
    next-best action: Investigate app/session drop-off; check for
    outages affecting this customer's channel." What is the work of
    the AI, and why train the model? Explain simply.
============================================================

TWO DIFFERENT THINGS ARE HAPPENING:

1) The AI's REAL job: turning messy evidence into a risk score
The AI looks at three types of raw evidence per customer:
- Text — their complaint/chat messages
- Voice — their call transcript
- Transaction — their banking behaviour (failed transfers, declines, etc.)

For each one, a trained model reads it and outputs a NUMBER (risk score)
and sometimes a CATEGORY (like "unfair_charge" or "card_declined"). A
fusion model then combines all three numbers into one final risk score
and figures out which evidence was the biggest driver ("dominant
modality"). That is the real AI work — reading thousands of complaints
and transcripts at scale, which no human could do by hand.

2) The "Recommended next-best action" text is NOT written by AI
That sentence comes from a plain lookup table in config.yaml, not from
the AI. The system just says: "the dominant signal was X, so print the
sentence written next to X in the table." A human wrote all those
sentences in advance. The AI only decided WHICH label applied.

WHY TRAIN THE MODEL AT ALL?
Training is how the AI LEARNS to read text/behaviour and assign a risk
number, instead of a human hand-writing rules like "if the complaint
contains 'refund', risk = 0.7."
- We gave it 900 example customers where the right answer was already
  known (dissatisfied or not).
- The model studied those examples repeatedly ("epochs") and adjusted
  itself to get better at guessing correctly.
- Now it can look at a NEW customer it has never seen and make a
  reasonable guess, because it learned the general pattern.

Without training, the model would just guess randomly — you can see
this in the training log: epoch 1 was worse (loss 0.668), epoch 3 was
better (loss 0.609) because it learned from the examples.

IN ONE SENTENCE: the AI's job is READING and SCORING each customer's
evidence; picking the ACTION SENTENCE is just a simple lookup table a
human already wrote.

Q3: Where is the data that was trained? What model trained the data?
    If an officer wants to input a new customer complaint, how can
    they do it — or is that not needed?
============================================================

1) WHERE IS THE TRAINING DATA?
Inside the project folder, in data/:
- data/raw/customers.jsonl — 900 sample ("synthetic") customer records:
  made-up complaint texts, call transcripts, and transaction numbers.
  NOT real bank data — the project rules forbid real customer data, so
  a script (ingest.py) invented realistic-looking sample customers.
- data/processed/customers.jsonl — the same 900 customers, cleaned up
  (names/emails removed) and with some data randomly deleted on purpose,
  to simulate "this customer never called in" etc.

So: the data is a sample, and it lives as plain text files in the project
folder.

2) WHAT MODEL TRAINED THE DATA?
Three small models, one per evidence type, plus one that combines them:

  Evidence type            | Model used
  --------------------------|--------------------------------------------
  Text (complaints/chat)    | DistilBERT — a pre-trained language AI,
                             | fine-tuned on the 900 examples
  Voice (call transcripts)  | Same DistilBERT model — voice is first
                             | converted to text (Whisper speech-to-text),
                             | then read the same way as text
  Transaction (behaviour)   | Logistic Regression — a simple, classic
                             | statistics model
  Combining all three       | Logistic Regression again — a small
                             | "fusion" model blending the 3 scores into
                             | 1 final risk score

Trained results are saved in models/: text_model, txn_model.joblib,
fusion_model.joblib. That's the "brain" — once trained it doesn't need
retraining every time, it just gets reused to score customers.

3) CAN AN OFFICER TYPE IN A NEW COMPLAINT?
No — not right now. The app only READS a pre-made file,
results/scored_customers.csv, created once by running evaluate.py on
the 900 fake customers. The dashboard is a READ-ONLY VIEWER of a fixed
batch. There is no text box for an officer to type a new complaint and
get a live score on this first page. (UPDATE: this is now possible on
the separate "Live Triage" page - see Q7. The officer can type a new
complaint there and the AI rates it straight away.)

Q4: For the demo project, in real life how is the device expected to
    get its data?
============================================================

The README explicitly marks real-world integrations as OUT OF SCOPE for
this academic build — it deliberately stops at "runnable locally with
sample data." But in an ACTUAL bank deployment, each evidence type would
come from an existing bank system:

  Evidence type          | Real-world source
  ------------------------|---------------------------------------------
  Text (complaints/chat)  | The bank's existing complaint/ticketing
                           | system, live chat logs, or emails — pulled
                           | automatically, not typed in by the officer
  Voice (calls)           | Call-centre recordings already stored by the
                           | phone/IVR system — AI runs speech-to-text
                           | on them automatically
  Transaction (behaviour) | The core banking system — records of failed
                           | transfers, card declines, app login activity
                           | — this data already exists in bank databases

HOW IT WOULD FLOW:
1. A background job (not a person) pulls new complaints, new call
   recordings, and recent transaction activity — on a schedule (e.g.
   nightly or hourly).
2. The AI scores every customer automatically, same as evaluate.py does
   now, just on real data instead of fake data.
3. The officer opens the dashboard each morning and sees a FRESH,
   AUTO-UPDATED ranked list — they never type anything in.

Honest way to describe this prototype: it proves the SCORING AND FUSION
LOGIC works, using synthetic data standing in for the three real feeds.
Hooking it up to a real complaint system, call-recording archive, and
core banking database is exactly the "production integration" work the
README marks out of scope for this academic build.

Q5: When the officer sees the score, what is their action? Is the
    score there to tell the officer how urgent the customer's
    complaint is?
============================================================

Yes — exactly. The score tells the officer HOW URGENT/CONCERNING a
customer is, so they know who to deal with first.

WHAT THE SCORE MEANS:
- Risk score (0 to 1) — how likely this customer is to be a serious
  problem (angry, about to leave the bank, victim of fraud, etc.).
  Higher = more urgent.
- Priority rank (#1, #2, #3...) — customers sorted by that score, so #1
  is the most urgent customer that day, #2 is next, and so on.

Think of it like a hospital triage nurse: the AI doesn't treat the
patient, it just says "this one needs a doctor now, that one can wait."

WHAT THE OFFICER ACTUALLY DOES WITH IT:
1. Open the dashboard — see the ranked list, top to bottom = most urgent
   to least urgent.
2. Start from the top. Open customer #1 first (highest risk).
3. Read the "why" — which evidence caused the high score (e.g. "this
   customer's complaint mentions an unauthorised deduction, and they've
   had 3 failed transfers this week").
4. Read the suggested action (e.g. "Escalate to fraud/dispute team for
   urgent investigation") — just a suggestion, the officer decides if it
   makes sense.
5. The officer does the REAL work themselves — calling the customer,
   checking their account, reversing a charge, escalating to fraud team,
   etc. THE AI DOES NONE OF THIS. It only pointed them to the right
   customer and gave a starting hint.
6. Mark it in the app — "Act on recommendation" or "Dismiss" — just for
   tracking, not because the app does anything automatically.
7. Move to the next customer down the list, repeat — working through
   urgent cases first instead of randomly or waiting for louder
   complaints.

THE KEY RULE (legal/ethical, baked into the whole project):
The AI NEVER contacts the customer, NEVER reverses a charge, NEVER
blocks a card by itself. It only answers one question: "out of all our
customers, who should a human look at first, and why?" Every actual
decision and action stays with the officer.

Q6: How does my client run this app on her own laptop, after she has
    installed Docker Desktop? (Simple steps, no coding knowledge needed)
============================================================

WHAT SHE NEEDS FIRST:
- A Windows or Mac laptop.
- Docker Desktop installed and opened at least once (so it has finished
  its first-time setup). Download link: https://www.docker.com/products/docker-desktop/
- The project folder (this whole folder) copied onto her laptop, e.g. on
  the Desktop.

STEP-BY-STEP:

1. START DOCKER DESKTOP
   Open the Docker Desktop app and wait until it says it is "running"
   (the whale icon in the taskbar/menu bar stops animating). Leave it
   open in the background — it must stay open the whole time she uses
   the app.

2. OPEN A TERMINAL IN THE PROJECT FOLDER
   - Windows: open the project folder in File Explorer, click into the
     address bar at the top, type "cmd" and press Enter — this opens a
     black command window already inside the right folder.
   - Mac: open Terminal, type "cd " (with a space, no quotes), drag the
     project folder into the Terminal window, then press Enter.

3. BUILD AND START THE APP
   In that terminal window, type this single command and press Enter:
       docker compose up
   The first time, this will take a few minutes — Docker is downloading
   everything the app needs and preparing the sample data. She will see
   a lot of text scroll by; that is normal. Wait until it stops
   scrolling and settles (it will mention "Streamlit" and a URL).

4. OPEN THE APP IN A BROWSER
   Open Chrome/Edge/Safari and go to:
       http://localhost:8502
   The "Multimodal Customer Experience Decision Support" dashboard
   should appear (see Q1 above for what to do once it's open).

5. WHEN SHE IS DONE FOR THE DAY
   Go back to the terminal window and press Ctrl + C to stop the app.
   Closing the terminal window also stops it. Docker Desktop itself can
   then be closed too.

6. TO RUN IT AGAIN LATER
   She does not need to repeat all the steps every time. Next time she
   wants to use the app, she only needs to:
   - Open Docker Desktop and wait for it to say "running"
   - Open a terminal in the project folder (step 2)
   - Type: docker compose up
   - Go to http://localhost:8502
   This restart is much faster than the first time, since everything is
   already downloaded and prepared.

THINGS THAT CAN GO WRONG:
- "docker: command not found" / "'docker' is not recognized" — Docker
  Desktop is not open yet, or needs a laptop restart after installing.
- Page won't load at localhost:8502 — check the terminal window is
  still open and hasn't shown an error; wait a little longer on first
  run since it trains the models before the page becomes available.
- "port is already allocated" / "Bind for 0.0.0.0:8501 failed" — another
  program on the laptop is already using that port. This project now
  uses port 8502 on the laptop (set in docker-compose.yml), so open
  http://localhost:8502. If 8502 is also taken, change the first number
  in the line "8502:8501" in docker-compose.yml to any free number.
- Nothing to type herself — she never needs to install Python, or any
  of the individual pieces (pandas, torch, etc). Docker packages all of
  that inside itself automatically.

Q7: What is the "Live Triage" page? How does a complaint come in
    and how does the AI agent rate it? (Simple explanation)
============================================================

WHY IT WAS ADDED
The first page (the ranked worklist) only shows a finished batch. It
cannot show a complaint ARRIVING and being rated. Live Triage fills
that gap so the demo shows the whole story: complaint in -> AI rates it
-> officer decides.

HOW TO OPEN IT
Start the app as usual (docker compose up, then http://localhost:8502).
In the LEFT SIDEBAR you will now see two pages. Click "Live Triage".
(The first time it takes about 25 seconds to load the trained models.)

WHAT IS THE "AI AGENT"?
Think of it as a junior analyst that handles ONE complaint step by
step, and shows its working:
  1. Receives the complaint (personal details like phone numbers and
     account numbers are removed first).
  2. Reads and classifies it (text model): what is it about, and how
     unhappy does the customer sound?
  3. Looks up the customer's transaction history (transaction model):
     failed transfers, card declines, changes in app use.
  4. If there is a call transcript, scores that too.
  5. Combines all the evidence into ONE risk score (fusion model).
     If some evidence is missing, it is NOT treated as zero - the
     weight moves to the evidence that is present.
  6. Gives a priority tier and a response-time target:
        RED    Critical - reply within 2 hours
        ORANGE High     - reply within 8 hours
        YELLOW Medium   - reply within 24 hours
        GREEN  Low      - reply within 72 hours
  7. Suggests the next action and drafts a reply to the customer.

IS AN LLM / CLAUDE / PAID SERVICE USED?
No. It uses the SAME trained models already in the models/ folder.
It is free, runs offline on the laptop, and gives the same answer
every time. The draft replies and next actions are ready-made
templates in config.yaml (a human wrote them), not AI-generated text.
The AI's real job is still reading and scoring; the templates only
turn that result into sentences.

HOW TO DEMONSTRATE IT
- "Simulate 1 complaint": a random customer and complaint (from the
  sample data) arrives. Watch the agent's steps appear one by one,
  then the case drops into the queue below.
- "Simulate 5 at once": fills the queue quickly. The queue sorts
  itself so the most urgent case is always at the top.
- "Type a complaint": type your own complaint, pick a customer (so the
  agent can look up their transactions, or choose "Unknown customer"
  to see it work without transaction history), pick the channel, and
  press "Send to the AI agent".
- Click a case to open it: you see the complaint, WHY it got that
  rating, the suggested action, the agent's full step-by-step trace,
  and an editable draft reply.
- The officer clicks Approve or Dismiss. This only records the
  decision on screen. NOTHING is sent to the customer and no account
  is changed - the AI recommends, the human decides.
- Sidebar: a switch to show the steps slowly (good for presenting) or
  instantly, and a button to clear the queue.

THINGS TO BE HONEST ABOUT WHEN PRESENTING
- The steps are shown slowly on purpose for the audience. The real
  scoring takes well under a second.
- The models were trained on invented (synthetic) complaints, so
  complaints worded very differently from the training examples may be
  rated less reliably. This is a proof of concept, not a bank-ready
  system.
- The customer look-up uses the sample customers, standing in for the
  bank's core banking system. In a real bank, complaints and
  transaction history would arrive automatically from those systems
  (see Q4).
- A harmless message from a customer with a bad transaction history
  can still rate Medium. That is the fusion working as designed:
  behaviour counts as well as words.

WHERE THE CODE IS
- app/pages/1_Live_Triage.py  the page the officer sees
- src/triage.py               the agent (steps, tiers, reply)
- config.yaml                 tiers, response times, reply templates
                              (editable without touching code)
- tests/test_triage.py        automatic checks for the agent
